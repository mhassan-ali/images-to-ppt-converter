Slide 02 | Core Concept

⚡ Why a SERIAL Bus in a PARALLEL Computer?

"The Elegant Paradox of Modern Computing"

❌ Problems with Parallel Bus

🔴
Clock Skew: 32+ wires travel different distances, bits arrive at different times causing sync failures
🔴
Crosstalk: Adjacent wires generate electromagnetic interference at high frequencies
🔴
Cable Bulk: 50+ pin connectors — thick, rigid, expensive cables
🔴
Length Limitation: Max ~18 inches for fast parallel (IEEE-1284)
🔴
Speed Wall: Plateaus around 133 MHz — cannot scale higher

✅ Serial Bus Solutions (USB)

🟢
No Skew: Single differential pair — one bit at a time, perfectly timed
🟢
Differential Signaling: D+ and D− carry opposite signals — noise cancels out
🟢
Thin Cables: Just 4 wires (USB 2.0) — compact, flexible, cheap
🟢
Longer Reach: Up to 5 meters without signal degradation
🟢
Scales to GHz: Clock embedded in data stream (CDR) — reaches 80 Gbps
💡 KEY INSIGHT: At high clock rates, sending 1 bit fast beats sending 32 bits slowly — serial WINS!

Slide 05 | USB Mechanism — Overview

⚙️ How USB Works — Architecture Overview

"One host to rule them all — 127 devices on a single bus"

🖥️ Host-Controlled Architecture

  • PC (Host) initiates ALL communication
  • Devices NEVER transmit without permission
  • Eliminates bus contention completely
  • Host polls devices on a schedule
  • Root Hub built into the host controller

🌳 Tiered Star Topology

  • Up to 127 devices per host controller
  • Max 7 tiers of hubs deep
  • Each hub is transparent to the host
  • Hot-plugging: devices auto-enumerate
  • Hubs can have up to 7 downstream ports

Slide 09 | Engineering Analysis

📈 Predicting Actual Data Transfer Rates

"Why your USB drive never hits the advertised speed"

MASTER FORMULA
Actual Throughput = Signaling Rate × Encoding Efficiency × (1 − Protocol Overhead)
Version Signaling Rate Encoding Encoding Efficiency Protocol Overhead Predicted Actual Real-World
USB 2.0 480 Mbps NRZI+bit stuff ~87% ~25% ~280-320 Mbps ~280 Mbps
USB 3.0 5 Gbps 8b/10b (80%) 80% ~15% ~3.2-3.4 Gbps ~3.2 Gbps
USB 3.1 10 Gbps 128b/132b (97%) 97% ~10% ~7.2-7.5 Gbps ~7.2 Gbps
USB4 40 Gbps 64b/66b (97%) 97% ~8% ~32-35 Gbps ~32 Gbps

⚠️ 8b/10b Encoding Loss

Sends 10 bits to carry 8 bits of data → 20% wasted! Used in USB 3.0 for robust clock recovery.

✅ 128b/132b Improvement

Sends 132 bits to carry 128 bits → only 3% waste! USB 3.1+ switched to this for 97% efficiency.

Slide 13 | Unique Applications

🚀 3 Unique USB Applications Parallel Buses Never Had

"Features that completely reinvented what a 'bus' can do"

⚡

USB Power Delivery

UNIQUE TO USB

Deliver up to 240W (48V × 5A) over the same data cable. Charges phones (15W), laptops (65W), even monitors (90W). Voltage negotiated dynamically (5V/9V/15V/20V/48V).

EU mandated USB-C as universal charger standard (2024)
🔄

USB On-The-Go (OTG)

UNIQUE TO USB

Your phone becomes the HOST! Connect USB drives, keyboards, MIDI controllers, or even Ethernet adapters directly to your phone — no PC required. Roles switch dynamically via ID pin.

Supported since USB 2.0 (2001 OTG supplement)
🖥️

USB4 Alternate Mode

UNIQUE TO USB4

One USB-C cable carries everything simultaneously: DisplayPort 2.1 (8K@60Hz video) + 40 Gbps data + 100W power. Replace 3 cables with 1. Enables one-cable docking stations.

Thunderbolt 4 uses USB4 with additional certification

Slide 15 | Conclusion

🏁 Key Takeaways

"Everything you need to remember about USB"

01
Serial buses beat parallel at high speeds by eliminating clock skew, crosstalk, and cable complexity — counterintuitive but proven
02
USB evolved from 1.5 Mbps (1996) → 80 Gbps (2024) — a 53,333× improvement in 28 years, doubling roughly every 2-3 years
03
USB's host-controlled, packet-based architecture with 4 transfer types (Control/Bulk/Interrupt/Isochronous) enables any device type
04
Actual throughput = Signaling Rate × Encoding Efficiency × Protocol Efficiency — encoding (8b/10b vs 128b/132b) is critical
05
USB uniquely enables Power Delivery (240W), OTG (role switching), and Alt-Mode (video+data+power in one cable) — impossible with parallel buses

"In a world of parallel processors, the serial bus proved that sometimes, less is more."
